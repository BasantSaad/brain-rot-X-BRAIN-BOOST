# Distraction And Risk Window Control

Tags: distraction, risk window, apps, social media, screen time, attention

A risk window is the time of day when distraction usually increases. It may happen after school, late at night, after meals, or when the student feels tired. The best response is not punishment; it is redesigning the environment during that window.

During a risk window, use shorter tasks, visible timers, app limits, and prepared alternatives. The student should know what to do before the distraction starts. A prepared alternative can be a five-minute reset, an easy revision task, or a short walk.

Screen time should be interpreted by trend, not by one isolated day. A single high-usage day may happen because of stress or free time. A rising seven-day trend is more important because it shows a pattern.

Brain boosting means reducing high-friction starts. Put the study material open before the session, silence nonessential notifications, and keep the first action obvious.
