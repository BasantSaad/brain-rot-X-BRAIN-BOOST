# Sleep And Cognitive Recovery

Tags: sleep, recovery, bedtime, attention, memory, night, calm

Sleep protects attention, memory consolidation, emotional control, and motivation. A student who sleeps poorly may look lazy, but the real problem can be weak recovery. The system should treat sleep as a core focus metric, not a side habit.

The last two hours before bedtime should reduce fast switching. Fast switching means jumping between short videos, social feeds, games, chats, and notifications. This pattern keeps the brain alert and delays sleep readiness.

A useful sleep protection technique is a "low stimulation runway": dim lights, reduce phone use, prepare tomorrow's materials, and choose calm tasks such as light review or planning. The goal is not perfect discipline; the goal is making sleep easier to start.

When sleep improves for several nights, study sessions can become longer. When sleep drops, the system should recommend shorter sessions and simpler tasks.
